# Exercise: explain it to someone who doesn't care how it works

**Time:** 20 minutes · **Work:** in threes (**explainer**, **stakeholder**, **observer**), rotating

## Warm-up: de-jargon (3 min, on your own)

Rewrite each one for a service manager. Keep it to one sentence, with no technical terms.

1. "The join fans out because the lookup isn't unique on the key."
2. "We quarantine rows that fail schema validation."
3. "The pipeline is idempotent, so we can backfill."
4. "Reconciliation balances but we've got a misallocation from the wrong lookup version."
5. "It's an SCD type 2 dimension."

## Role-play: 3 rounds × 5 min

Each round:
1. The **stakeholder** picks a card from `role_cards.md` and reads it privately.
2. The **explainer** has **3 minutes** to explain **the session 15 pipeline** (school attendance, or your own design) to that stakeholder.
3. The stakeholder **stays in role**: interrupt, ask what *they* care about, push back.
4. The **observer** fills in `feedback_form.md`. Then **1 minute** of feedback: one thing that worked, one thing to try.

Rotate roles and use a different card each round.

## Things to try
- **Start with what they care about**, not with how it works.
- **Lead with the answer:** "Yes, Thursday publication is achievable, with one risk."
- Use **one concrete example** rather than a general description.
- **Check understanding:** "Does that answer what you needed?"
- If asked something you don't know, say so: "I don't know yet. I'll find out by Tuesday."

## Discuss afterwards
- Which stakeholder was hardest to explain to? Why?
- What did you leave out, and was that right?
